TrueCast Privacy Notice

Version: 2.0 Last updated: October 26, 2026

TrueCast is made by Gridion Works ("we", "us"). This notice explains what TrueCast does with your information. It's built into the app, so you can read it offline at any time in Settings > About. It's also online at https://christellez173.github.io/truecast-legal/privacy.

1. Our privacy commitment

TrueCast is local-first and private by design. It's a casting hub and browser that works on your phone and your home Wi-Fi. It has no ads and no ad trackers, we don't sell your data, and we don't watch what you do. There are no TrueCast accounts and no TrueCast database of your data. Nothing about your browsing, your media or your settings is sent to us. The only thing that can ever reach us is a crash report, and only if you choose to email it to us yourself (see section 4).

2. What we don't collect

  • Camera and microphone. TrueCast doesn't request camera or microphone permission and can't use them. Websites can't use your camera or microphone through TrueCast.
  • Voice search: if you tap the mic in the Browser's search bar, Android opens your phone's own speech service (usually Google's). That service listens under its own privacy policy and gives TrueCast only the text. TrueCast uses the text as your search or web address.
  • Your photos and videos. You choose them with Android's picker, so TrueCast can only open the files you pick. TrueCast doesn't upload them to us or to the internet. To play one on a TV, TrueCast sends it from your phone straight to that TV over your Wi-Fi.
  • Your screen. "Mirror phone" only opens Android's own Cast or Smart View screen-mirroring setting. Android and your TV do the mirroring; TrueCast doesn't capture or record your screen or sound.
  • Your browsing. We don't receive, track or sync your logins, passwords or browsing history. TrueCast has no account and no sync.
  • Normal tabs work like any browser: websites can save cookies and site data, and TrueCast remembers your open tabs, your recent sites on the Browser home and small tab previews. All of this stays on your phone.
  • Private tabs each get their own separate storage, which is deleted when you close the tab (and any leftovers are cleaned up the next time TrueCast starts). Private tabs aren't saved as tabs, recent sites or previews, and videos from private tabs aren't saved in layouts, queues or your recent files.

3. What stays on your device

  • Finding TVs: TrueCast looks for Chromecast (and other Google Cast) devices and Roku devices on your Wi-Fi network.
  • Videos you cast: video links that TrueCast finds on a page are passed to the TV you chose, and the TV plays the video directly from the website. Videos you add to a queue or a saved layout keep their link on your phone.
  • Downloads: when you download a video from a page, TrueCast saves a copy inside its own app storage on your phone (in a TrueCast/Downloads folder) and shows it under Files > Downloads. It isn't sent anywhere. A download started from a private tab is shown only as "Private video", is never added to your recent files, and its details aren't kept after the app closes.
  • Captions: captions you pick are kept on your phone for that video. To show them on the TV, TrueCast turns them into a caption file and serves it from your phone to the TV over your Wi-Fi, the same way it serves your videos.
  • Settings and setups: your settings, saved Studio layouts, video queues, recent-file list (name, size and a small thumbnail) and open normal tabs are stored on your phone.
  • Crash reports: saved on your phone only. See section 4.
  • Your consent record: which versions of this notice and the terms you accepted, and when. It stays on your phone.
  • Deleting your data: uninstalling TrueCast, or clearing its storage in Android Settings > Apps > TrueCast > Storage, deletes everything TrueCast stored, including downloads and crash reports. You can also delete layouts, queue items, recent sites, tabs, downloads and crash reports inside the app.

4. Crash reports

If TrueCast closes unexpectedly, it saves a crash report on your phone only. Only the last 5 are kept.

A report contains exactly this:

  • the time (with its offset from UTC, not your time zone name)
  • the kind of problem
  • the app version name and code
  • your phone model and your Android version
  • the name of the screen you were on
  • whether a download was running
  • Android's own exit reason, when it gives one
  • the names of the app's screens and components, when a screen failed to draw
  • the error's technical trace (function and class names and line numbers)
  • the last 50 lines of the app's own log

Cleaning. Before anything is saved, TrueCast tries to remove web addresses, email addresses, IP and other network addresses, passwords, tokens, keys, cookies, Wi-Fi names and pairing codes, file names and folder paths, the names of your Cast devices (only the device type is kept) and long pieces of text. The app's log lines are written to leave out page titles, video titles, page text and your queue.

This cleaning is automatic and not perfect. It can miss, for example, part of a password or Wi-Fi name that has spaces in it, a code written as plain text, some unusual spellings of keys, a web address written without http:// or deliberately disguised, and short pieces of text in an error message. That's why you can always read a report before sending it.

Nothing is sent unless you choose to. A report only leaves your phone if you tap Send report (in the crash message) or Share (in Settings > Crash reports). That opens an email draft in your own email app, addressed to truecast-crash@mail.grokbot.com, with the report as the message. You then decide whether to send it. If no email app can take it, Android's share sheet opens instead and you choose where it goes. If you send it, we receive the report plus whatever your email service includes, such as your email address. You can read or delete saved reports at any time in Settings > Crash reports.

5. Other companies and websites

  • Websites you open have their own privacy policies. Like any browser, TrueCast connects you to them directly, so they see things like your IP address and browser details. We don't broker, log or intercept that traffic.
  • Search: text you type in the address bar that isn't a web address, and voice search text, is sent to Google Search.
  • OpenSubtitles: to find captions, you sign in with your own OpenSubtitles account. TrueCast sends your username and password only to OpenSubtitles to sign you in, and doesn't keep your password. It stores the sign-in token OpenSubtitles returns in secure storage on your phone only, and sends that token only to OpenSubtitles. Signing out in Settings > Subtitles deletes it. We never receive your login or token. When you search for captions, TrueCast sends OpenSubtitles.com a search made from the video's name (taken from its file name or title with quality tags removed) and, when it can tell, the year, season and episode number, plus the caption language (English). When you pick a result, TrueCast asks OpenSubtitles for that file and downloads it. Like any internet request, OpenSubtitles sees your IP address. Your video file itself is never sent. OpenSubtitles' own privacy policy applies. Caption search results come from OpenSubtitles.com, and TrueCast credits them in the captions sheet and in Settings > About.
  • Your TV: the Chromecast or Roku device you cast to gets the video link or file (and any captions) and plays it. The TV maker's own privacy policy applies to what the TV does.
  • Google Cast: TrueCast uses Google's Cast SDK and Google Play services to talk to Cast devices. The Cast SDK collects device and diagnostic information (such as how casting is used, how it performs and any errors) and shares it with Google, under Google's privacy policy. Google collects this, not us, and we don't receive it.
  • Your email app: if you send a crash report, your email provider handles that message under its own policy.

6. Security

  • There are no TrueCast accounts and no TrueCast database of your data.
  • Your data stays in TrueCast's private app storage on your phone. Other apps can't read it.
  • TV remote commands: Roku remote commands go straight from your phone to your TV over your local Wi-Fi without encryption, so others on the same network could see them. For Chromecast, the connection runs through Google Play services.
  • Photos, videos and caption files served from your phone to a TV travel over your Wi-Fi without encryption, at a hard-to-guess address that only works while you're casting.
  • The built-in browser doesn't let websites read your files, open extra windows or use your location.

7. Children

TrueCast isn't designed for children under 13. We don't collect personal information from anyone, including children.

8. Changes to this notice

When this notice changes, we'll update the version and the "Last updated" date at the top. The new version comes with an app update, and TrueCast asks you to read and accept it again the first time you open the app after a change.
